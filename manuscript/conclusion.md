Tapestry closes the gap between what PacBio HiFi sequencing measures — a methylation call at every covered CpG — and what genome-wide discovery of homolog-specific methylation lesions requires: that call resolved onto a specific parental homolog, traceable across a pedigree to a founder of origin. It does so without any locus-specific assay, recovering haplotype transmission from the inheritance structure of unphased joint-called variants and using it to rebucket read-backed methylation tracks onto founder-labelled ones. The primary product, a per-CpG BED of founder-phased methylation, turns both established classes of disease-causing lesion — homolog-specific loss of methylation at an imprinted locus, and the compound genetic-epigenetic heterozygote — into short queries that run over every covered CpG rather than a pre-selected panel.

Tapestry is limited in that it phases methylation only within hap-map blocks, so coverage of the genome is set by HiFi read length, mappability, heterozygous-variant density, and the haplotype structure of the particular pedigree. 

The discovery scenarios are demonstrated here as queries against the BED, not yet as findings in a disease cohort. The natural next step is to apply tapestry to pedigrees segregating an undiagnosed phenotype and ask whether a parent-of-origin-restricted methylation lesion, or a methylation lesion in trans with a coding loss-of-function, segregates with disease — extending the small catalog of known homolog-specific methylation disorders into the much larger catalog of loci where such lesions may act but no targeted assay yet exists.
