PacBio HiFi sequencing can now report DNA methylation status at every CpG site in the human genome. This advance opens a new diagnostic and research frontier in familial disease: lesions that carry no sequence change but instead consist of an altered methylation state on a single physical homolog — invisible to standard sequencing, and indistinguishable from background by pooled-haplotype methylation. Two classes of homolog-specific lesion share this signature. The first is a de novo methylation change on a single homolog. The most prominent clinical example is maternal loss of methylation at an imprinted locus, the leading epigenetic cause of imprinting disorders (e.g. Beckwith-Wiedemann and Angelman syndromes); gain-of-methylation events and other epimutations fall in the same class and are of broader research interest. The second is a compound genetic-epigenetic heterozygote, in which a coding variant on one parental homolog sits *in trans* with an epimutation silencing the other (e.g. Lynch syndrome, hereditary breast and ovarian cancer). Detecting either class requires the methylation state at each CpG on one specific physical homolog, with that homolog traceable to a parent of origin. We present Tapestry, a pipeline that phases per-CpG methylation from PacBio HiFi reads onto the founder haplotypes of a human pedigree (including the trio special case), and demonstrate how its output surfaces both classes of lesion through short downstream queries. Tapestry is available at https://github.com/quinlan-lab/tapestry.
