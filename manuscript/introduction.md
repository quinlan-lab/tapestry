Genome-wide CpG-methylation assays let researchers studying disease in families search for loci where a methylation change on a single homolog causes disease — on its own, or in trans with a genetic mutation on the other homolog. Unphased methylation level - the fraction of reads overlapping a site that carry a methylation mark at that site - integrates over both homologs. Accordingly, a *heterozygously methylated* site — methylated on one homolog, unmethylated on the other — takes an intermediate value (Fig 1A), indistinguishable from a homozygously partially-methylated site, such as arises from somatic mosaicism or incomplete maintenance methylation. Partitioning reads by haplotype — enabled by PacBio HiFi over long read-backed phase blocks — splits the pooled methylation track in two and resolves the heterozygous state (Fig 1B). If we can also trace each of these two haplotypes back to the parental homolog it descends from, we can discover loci whose disease-causing lesion is invisible to sequencing: a haplotype-specific change in methylation, not in sequence. 

Consider first the most common epigenetic cause of human imprinting disorders — a maternal-haplotype loss of methylation (LOM) at an imprinted locus (Fig 1C) ([Monk et al. 2019, Nat Rev Genet](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-018-0092-0)). Maternal LOM at the *KCNQ1OT1* locus accounts for roughly half of Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome cases ([Brioude et al. 2018, Nat Rev Endocrinol](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrendo.2017.166)). Maternal LOM at the *PLAGL1* locus underlies transient neonatal diabetes mellitus ([Temple & Mackay, GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1534/)). Maternal LOM at the *GNAS* locus underlies pseudohypoparathyroidism ([Mantovani et al. 2018, Nat Rev Endocrinol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-018-0042-0)). Maternal LOM at the *MEG3/DLK1* locus underlies Temple syndrome ([Ioannides et al. 2014, J Med Genet](DOI-TO-VERIFY)). Maternal LOM at the *SNRPN* locus causes the imprinting-defect form of Angelman syndrome ([Buiting et al. 2003, Am J Hum Genet](DOI-TO-VERIFY)). And LOM across several maternally methylated loci is the predominant signature of multi-locus imprinting disturbance ([Mackay et al. 2008, Nat Genet](DOI-TO-VERIFY)). Carrying no sequence change, the lesion is invisible to sequencing-based diagnostics; lying on one parental homolog, it is equally invisible to pooled methylation, whose ~50%→~0% drop cannot distinguish maternal LOM from a paternal LOM at a paternally methylated locus or from biallelic hypomethylation — yet only maternal LOM is disease-causing, and the parent of origin determines the syndrome. Detecting it requires comparing the child's methylation on the maternally transmitted homolog with that same homolog in the mother. 

Consider next the compound genetic–epigenetic heterozygote (Fig 1D), in which a gene is inactivated by a sequence variant on one haplotype and an epimutation on the other. The Knudson two-hit model classically requires two coding lesions in trans, but the second hit can be epigenetic: in Lynch syndrome and in hereditary breast and ovarian cancer, patients carry a germline loss-of-function variant in *MLH1*, *MSH2*, or *BRCA1* on one allele and promoter hypermethylation silencing the other ([Hitchins 2015, Nat Rev Cancer](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrc3989)). The pattern extends beyond cancer: in the recessive disorder cblC, a coding loss-of-function in *MMACHC* sits in trans with an epimutation hypermethylating the other *MMACHC* allele, itself driven by an inherited variant in the neighbouring gene *PRDX1* ([Guéant et al. 2018, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02306-5)). More generally, any recessive disease whose proband carries a single coding loss-of-function is missed by sequencing alone if the second allele is silenced epigenetically rather than mutated. Detecting these cases requires phasing both the variant and the methylation call to a parent of origin and asking whether they sit in trans.

Both classes share a common diagnostic requirement that standard short- and long-read pipelines fail to meet: the methylation state at a CpG on *one specific physical homolog*, with that homolog traceable across generations to a parent of origin. Tapestry is a workflow that resolves this diagnostic gap for every CpG in every member of a sequenced pedigree, enabling both classes of discovery in a single pipeline.
