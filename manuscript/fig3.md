**Figure 3. Reconstructing founder-labeled haplotype sequences within an IBD segment.** Panels A–D set up the harder case — phasing alleles at a *non-informative site* by exhaustive enumeration — and panel E then composes that result with the direct read-off at informative sites (Fig 2) to recover, for each kid, the full bitstring on each of its two carried founder haplotypes. **(A–D)** At a non-informative site (a site at which both parents are heterozygous, so the parental genotypes alone don't determine which allele lies on which parental haplotype), the site can still be phased within an IBD segment (defined in Fig 2) by exhaustively enumerating every assignment of alleles (0 and 1) to the parental haplotypes — A, B for dad and C, D for mom — that is consistent with the parents' observed unphased genotypes (four such assignments at a non-informative site, since each parent is 0/1), and choosing the assignment that best explains the kids' observed unphased genotypes given each kid's phased founder-haplotype label (paternal letter | maternal letter), which is constant within the segment and inferred upstream from informative sites. For each assignment we count the kids whose expected unphased genotype matches the observed (# Matches). The assignment with the most matches (boxed) gives the deduced phased genotypes for the kids. Algorithm inputs are coloured red. **(E)** Applying the site-wise reconstruction logic to every site in an IBD segment recovers, for each kid, the full bitstring on each of its two carried founder haplotypes — labelled `pat_X` (dad's transmitted founder) and `mat_Y` (mom's transmitted founder), matching the row conventions used in Fig 4A. Sites in the segment fall into two classes, marked column-by-column beneath the rows by a filled (●) or open (○) dot (legend at right): at *informative* sites (●) one parent is homozygous, and the bit on each kid's `pat_X` / `mat_Y` is read off directly from the parental genotypes (Fig 2); at *non-informative* sites (○) both parents are heterozygous, and the bit is accessible only through the exhaustive enumeration of panels A–D of this figure. The two site classes are intermingled along the segment in real data, as shown. Together, Fig 2 and Fig 3 cover every site in the segment. Because each founder bitstring is a property of the parent rather than the kid, kids that share a founder share that bitstring (here Kid1 and Kid3 both carry paternal founder A, and Kid2 and Kid3 both carry maternal founder D). These reconstructed pat/mat bitstrings are the inputs to the founder-phased-methylation matching used in Fig 4A.
