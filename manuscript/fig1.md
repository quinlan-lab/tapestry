**Figure 1. Phasing CpG methylation uncovers de novo methylation changes and compound heterozygote gene silencing.** **(A)** Single individual, before phasing. Aligned reads carry a 0/1 glyph at SNV columns and red (methylated) or blue (unmethylated) boxes at CpG columns. The sequence of 0s and 1s on each read defines the haplotype that the read belongs to. A single gray bigwig-style track reports the methylation level at each CpG site, defined as the fraction of reads at that site that are methylated. Since reads are pooled over the haplotypes, their methylation levels are averaged into one number, and heterozygous methylation (defined as hypermethylation on one haplotype and hypomethylation on the other) is hidden inside an intermediate value (e.g., 3/7 at the highlighted cluster). **(B)** Same reads, after phasing: reads are partitioned paternal-above-maternal using SNV bits carried by each read (read-backed phasing), and the pooled methylation track splits into two stacked per-haplotype bigwig tracks. Heterozygous methylation now resolves cleanly, e.g., into 0/4 on Hap1 versus 3/3 on Hap2. **(C)** De novo epimutation in a trio. Each individual's two homologs are drawn as horizontal coloured bars decorated with methylation lollipops at CpG positions (filled = methylated, open = unmethylated); the four founder haplotypes are labelled A/B (dad) and C/D (mom). The kid's paternal homolog (A, inherited from dad) is methylated at the right CpG cluster, but dad's same physical homolog A is unmethylated there: a de novo gain of methylation across a single meiosis (dashed box). Symmetrically on the maternal side (C from mom), the kid is unmethylated where mom is methylated — a de novo loss. De novo events are detectable only when parent and child can be compared on the same haplotype, which requires (transmission-based) phased methylation. **(D)** Compound genetic-epigenetic heterozygote in a trio. The kid inherits dad's hap A — which carries a frameshift SNV (red star) that triggers nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) of the transcript from that allele — and mom's hap C, which carries an aberrantly hyper-methylated promoter that silences transcription from that allele. The two hits sit on opposite parental haplotypes, so both copies of the gene are inactivated in trans, while each parent is a silent carrier of just one hit. Neither hit is visible to an unphased genotype call or an unphased methylation call alone.
