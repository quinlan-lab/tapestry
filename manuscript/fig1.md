**Figure 1. Phasing CpG methylation uncovers de novo methylation changes and compound heterozygote gene silencing.** **(A)** Single individual, before phasing. Reads carry a 0/1 glyph at SNV columns and red/blue boxes at methylated/unmethylated CpGs. The gray track reports pooled per-CpG methylation; averaging both haplotypes hides heterozygous methylation in an intermediate value (3/7, highlighted cluster). **(B)** Same reads, after phasing. SNV bits partition reads hap1-above-hap2 (read-backed phasing); the pooled track splits in two and heterozygous methylation resolves cleanly (0/4 vs. 3/3). The two-tone grey bar marks the *read-backed phase block*; the founder identity (paternal vs. maternal) of hap1/hap2 is assigned downstream (Fig 4). **(C)** Maternal loss of methylation (LOM) at an imprinted locus in a trio. Homologs are drawn as coloured bars with methylation lollipops at CpGs (filled = methylated, open = unmethylated); the four founder haplotypes are labelled A/B (dad) and C/D (mom). Both parents show normal imprinting of this maternally methylated locus. The kid inherits A from dad (unmethylated, as expected) and C from mom, but C in the kid is unmethylated rather than methylated (dashed box) — a maternal LOM lesion. See main text for disease relevance and why detection requires parent-of-origin–phased methylation. **(D)** Compound genetic-epigenetic heterozygote in a trio. The kid inherits dad's hap A, carrying a frameshift SNV (red star), and mom's hap C, carrying an aberrantly hyper-methylated promoter — two hits on opposite parental haplotypes that inactivate the gene in trans, while each parent is a silent carrier of one hit. See main text for why neither hit is visible to unphased genotype or methylation calls alone.
