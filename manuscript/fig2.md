**Figure 2. Tracking the transmission of haplotypes in a nuclear family.** **(A)** Ground truth: four founder haplotypes (Dad A, Dad B, Mom C, Mom D) as 0/1 cells (0 = REF, 1 = ALT at an SNV), followed by each kid's two phased homologs (paternal "p" above maternal "m"), every cell coloured by the founder it was inherited from. Kid3's paternal row switches A→B mid-locus, marking a single paternal recombination. **(B)** Maternal-side reconstruction. *(i)* Unphased VCF genotypes restricted to *mom-informative* sites. *(ii)* The per-site maternally transmitted allele. *(iii)* Per-site partitioning of kids into majority class "C" and minority class "D". *(iv)* Collapsed maternal-haplotype segments per kid. **(C)** Paternal-side reconstruction; tables *(i–iii)* mirror panel B with A/B labels. The red rectangle in (iii) marks the putative double crossover; at every informative site to its right, swapping the kids' A/B labels (so the kid previously labelled A is relabelled B and vice versa, preserving the per-site partitioning) yields the scenario in *(iv)*, collapsed to per-kid paternal segments in *(v)*. **(D)** *IBD (identity-by-descent) segments.* For each kid, the pipe (`|`) separates the paternal founder (left) from the maternal founder (right) — a convention re-used in Figs 3 and 4.
