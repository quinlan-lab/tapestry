Figure 1. Why phase DNA methylation, and what tapestry's haplotype-resolved BED enables. (A) A small synthetic locus with mixed heterozygous SNVs and CpGs. Ten ragged reads cover overlapping windows; each read is one row carrying a 0/1 glyph at SNV columns and a small red (unmethylated) or blue (methylated) box at CpG columns. Above the pile-up, a single gray bigwig-style track reports the pooled per-CpG methylation level. Reads are unpartitioned: the two haplotypes' methylation states are averaged into a single number per CpG, and per-CpG heterozygous methylation is hidden inside an intermediate value (the discordant region marked beneath the bigwig). (B) The same ten reads, now partitioned by haplotype using the SNV bits as a phasing signal. Rows are grouped paternal-above-maternal, and the pooled bigwig splits into two stacked per-haplotype tracks. The discordant region resolves into a methylated maternal haplotype and an unmethylated paternal one. What was an intermediate signal becomes two interpretable signals. (C) The single individual is now placed into a trio. Each member's two haplotypes are drawn as horizontal coloured bars decorated with methylation lollipops (filled = methylated, open = unmethylated). At the right CpG cluster, two de novo events sit on opposite parental haplotypes: the kid's paternal homolog (A, inherited from dad) gains methylation relative to dad, and the kid's maternal homolog (C, inherited from mom) loses methylation relative to mom. The left cluster is concordant on both sides. (D) A few rows of the haplotype-resolved BED that tapestry produces for this locus. Columns name the kid's paternal-side methylation level (kid_pat), the founder letter the kid inherited on the paternal side (pat_hap), and the methylation level on each of dad's two haplotypes (dad_A, dad_B). The two highlighted rows mark the right CpG cluster where the de novo gain occurs. (E) A two-step polars query that surfaces the de novo gain in panel D from a generic haplotype-resolved BED. Step 1 selects the dad haplotype that was transmitted to the kid using the pat_hap column; step 2 keeps CpGs where kid_pat - dad_transmitted is positive on average over a small window. Mirroring the column choice and the sign exposes the symmetric scenarios (de novo loss; maternal-side events). (F) Same trio, different scenario: the kid inherits dad's A — which carries a SNV variant (red star) — and mom's C — which carries an aberrantly hyper-methylated promoter region. The two hits sit on opposite parental haplotypes, so the kid is a compound genetic-epigenetic heterozygote in trans: silenced on the maternal allele by aberrant methylation, broken on the paternal allele by a coding variant. Each parent is a silent carrier of one of the two hits, and an unphased view would miss the trans configuration entirely.
